Staff propose cutting permit fees by at least half for apartment buildings of five storeys or more

Agenda item 7.a

Council will consider on Monday a staff recommendation to cut building permit fees for apartment buildings of five storeys or more. Permits issued from January 1 to December 31, 2027 would be charged a fee based on building size that works out to at least 50% less than current fees. The report describes this as a one-time, additional incentive.

The aim, the report says, is to help the City meet its Housing Accelerator Fund targets. Under its agreement, the City must reach a target for new housing units, along with several other housing targets, by the end of 2027 to qualify for the program's 2028 payment of $2.02 million. In 2025, new housing units by building permit came in at 138% of the first-year target, and the report says that, overall, development is on pace for meeting the 2026 amount. The report, which gives its status as of August 2026, says the targets for affordable units and missing middle housing have already been achieved, but that the City is "extremely low" on new apartment units of five storeys or more: 46 achieved against a target of 315 (15%). The report says the larger yearly target falls in 2027 and that, as of August 2026, more than 50% of the housing target remained to be achieved.

Staff estimate the reduction at $50,000 to $90,000 per permit, depending on the size of the building, and say it may help two to four permits from projects they know of. The report notes that the incentive alone may not trigger a new permit.

Council directed staff in September 2024 to proceed with an application for the fund's second round, and received a progress report on the City's action plan in November 2025.
