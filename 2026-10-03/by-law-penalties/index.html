Council asked to approve, in principle, a City system for by-law penalties

Agenda item 19.b

Staff are recommending that council approve in principle an Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) for some municipal by-law contraventions.

Today, the staff report says, by-law charges and parking tickets go to provincial offences court, administered by Elgin County. Under an AMPS, "minor violations are instead handled through an internal administrative process," the report says. Staff describe this as "an efficient, cost-effective, and streamlined process" for the City and for people who receive a ticket. A person who receives a penalty would have the right to ask for a review or hearing before a Screening or Hearing Officer. A Screening Officer could affirm, cancel or reduce the penalty, or extend the time to pay.

The report lists the Traffic and Parking, Animal Control, Lot Maintenance and Noise Control by-laws as priorities for initial inclusion.

On cost, the report estimates about $15,500 in one-time start-up costs and about $8,300 a year for software licensing and maintenance. It puts what the City now spends under the current system, mainly software licensing and administration, at about $13,535 a year. Those figures are not like for like: the report also lists staff time for court preparation and collections under the current system without a dollar amount. Hearing Officers typically cost $300 per half day or $600 per day, the report says, and reusing current e-ticketing hardware could save about $4,700. It anticipates the costs can be met within the City's existing budget, and says any net financial impact will be refined as the by-law is developed. It does not give penalty amounts or a date for the draft by-law.

Approval on Monday would not start the system. Staff would first review the City's by-laws and develop a framework, then bring a draft by-law and procedures back to council for approval before it takes effect.
