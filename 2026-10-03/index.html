Staff propose cutting permit fees by at least half for apartment buildings of five storeys or more

Agenda item 7.a

Council will consider on Monday a staff recommendation to cut building permit fees for apartment buildings of five storeys or more. Permits issued from January 1 to December 31, 2027 would be charged a fee based on building size that works out to at least 50% less than current fees. The report describes this as a one-time, additional incentive.

The aim, the report says, is to help the City meet its Housing Accelerator Fund targets. Under its agreement, the City must reach a target for new housing units, along with several other housing targets, by the end of 2027 to qualify for the program's 2028 payment of $2.02 million. In 2025, new housing units by building permit came in at 138% of the first-year target, and the report says that, overall, development is on pace for meeting the 2026 amount. The report, which gives its status as of August 2026, says the targets for affordable units and missing middle housing have already been achieved, but that the City is "extremely low" on new apartment units of five storeys or more: 46 achieved against a target of 315 (15%). The report says the larger yearly target falls in 2027 and that, as of August 2026, more than 50% of the housing target remained to be achieved.

Staff estimate the reduction at $50,000 to $90,000 per permit, depending on the size of the building, and say it may help two to four permits from projects they know of. The report notes that the incentive alone may not trigger a new permit.

Council directed staff in September 2024 to proceed with an application for the fund's second round, and received a progress report on the City's action plan in November 2025.

Council to receive a report on a new Noise Control By-law

Agenda item 19.a

Council will consider a staff recommendation on Monday that report CC-20-26 on a new Noise Control By-law "be received for information" and that the by-law "be brought forward for Council’s consideration." The recommendation does not ask council to adopt the by-law. The draft leaves its effective date blank.

The report calls the City's current noise by-law, adopted in December 2020, "legally valid and enforceable," but says it takes a more traditional approach. Staff list the "Removal of quantitative noise limits and the adoption of qualitative approaches to enforcement." In the draft, whether something is noise turns on an officer's opinion that it "is likely to disturb the reasonable enjoyment of a Premises." It covers sound or vibration "audible at a Point of Reception."

The report says the change "would therefore result in operational cost savings" and "eliminates the need for enforcement staff to use specialized sound level meters." Its cost table, "Cost Breakdown – Quantitative Noise Reader Program," shows $26,393.62 in the first year and $2,400 a year after. It also says "There is no financial impact or additional costs associated with this update."

Under the draft, construction equipment noise that is clearly audible at a point of reception is deemed likely to disturb during restricted times: 9 p.m. to 7 a.m. Monday to Friday, 9 p.m. to 8 a.m. on Saturday, and 8 p.m. to 9 a.m. on Sundays and statutory holidays. While a Heat Warning is in effect, the equipment may start up to one hour earlier. The report says most set fines "have been standardized at $500.00"; it proposes a $250 set fine for persistent barking, calling or whining by a domestic animal. The report does not give the current by-law's hours or fines in the material we reviewed, so we cannot say how these compare.

Council asked to approve, in principle, a City system for by-law penalties

Agenda item 19.b

Staff are recommending that council approve in principle an Administrative Monetary Penalty System (AMPS) for some municipal by-law contraventions.

Today, the staff report says, by-law charges and parking tickets go to provincial offences court, administered by Elgin County. Under an AMPS, "minor violations are instead handled through an internal administrative process," the report says. Staff describe this as "an efficient, cost-effective, and streamlined process" for the City and for people who receive a ticket. A person who receives a penalty would have the right to ask for a review or hearing before a Screening or Hearing Officer. A Screening Officer could affirm, cancel or reduce the penalty, or extend the time to pay.

The report lists the Traffic and Parking, Animal Control, Lot Maintenance and Noise Control by-laws as priorities for initial inclusion.

On cost, the report estimates about $15,500 in one-time start-up costs and about $8,300 a year for software licensing and maintenance. It puts what the City now spends under the current system, mainly software licensing and administration, at about $13,535 a year. Those figures are not like for like: the report also lists staff time for court preparation and collections under the current system without a dollar amount. Hearing Officers typically cost $300 per half day or $600 per day, the report says, and reusing current e-ticketing hardware could save about $4,700. It anticipates the costs can be met within the City's existing budget, and says any net financial impact will be refined as the by-law is developed. It does not give penalty amounts or a date for the draft by-law.

Approval on Monday would not start the system. Staff would first review the City's by-laws and develop a framework, then bring a draft by-law and procedures back to council for approval before it takes effect.

City employee benefits contracts up for award

Agenda item 19.d

Council will be asked on Monday to award the City's employee and retiree group benefits contracts from January 1, 2027. Staff's report says the carrier search (a request for proposals, or RFP) delivers an overall 10.4% cost reduction, or $747,648 a year, while short-term disability rates rise 19.8%, or $7,306 a month.

Staff recommend that council award the accidental death and dismemberment contract to AIG Insurance, and the life, dependent life, short-term disability, long-term disability, extended health care and dental care contracts to Manulife Financial. These are the City's current carriers. The report says the City's last search for carriers was in 2021, and that quotes for all benefits were received from Manulife, Sun Life and Desjardins.

The report's table lists each benefit's "Rate Change (%)" and monthly dollar change. Short-term disability is the only one of the table's seven lines that rises, and it carries a 12-month rate guarantee.

Staff also ask council to approve the consultant's recommendation to move short-term disability, extended health care and dental benefits from "Retention Accounting" to "Administrative Services Only" (ASO). The report says that under Retention Accounting the City is already responsible for any deficit incurred, and that under ASO the insurer holds none of the reserve funds the report lists, because the policyholder is fully responsible for paying any deficit.

In November 2024, council approved a renewal of its Manulife benefit program for extended health care, dental, short-term disability, life insurance and long-term disability, "effective January 1, 2025, for a period of one (1) year." In December 2025, it received a report on the 2026 renewal.

How voting works in the October 26 election

Agenda item 19.c

A report from the Deputy City Clerk on Monday's council agenda, for information, sets out how voting works in the October 26 municipal election.

  • How to vote: electors can vote by internet or telephone, 24 hours a day, from 10 a.m. on October 13 to 8 p.m. on October 26.
  • Who can vote: the report says you must be 18 or older on October 26; a Canadian citizen; a resident, an owner or tenant of land in St. Thomas, or the spouse of such an owner or tenant; and not prohibited from voting or otherwise prohibited by law.
  • The voters' list: a name must be on it to vote. Check at https://stthomas.voterservices.ca. If you are not on the list, or need a correction (address, school support), submit Form EL15 to the City Clerk's Department: bring it, signed, with identification showing your name and qualifying address, to the Clerk's Office or a Voter Assistance Centre.
  • Voter Information Letters, with your PIN, are to arrive by mail by October 7.
  • Voter Assistance Centres:
    • Thursday, October 15, 1 to 7 p.m., Elgin Centre, 417 Wellington Street
    • Wednesday, October 21, noon to 4 p.m., St. Thomas Seniors' Centre, 225 Chestnut Street
    • Saturday, October 24, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., Elgin Centre
    • Monday, October 26, 10 a.m. to 8 p.m., St. Thomas Public Library, 153 Curtis Street
  • Institutions: special voting hours will be available in institutions where 20 or more beds are occupied by persons who are disabled, chronically ill, or infirm, and in long-term care or retirement homes where 50 or more beds are occupied. The report gives no dates.

The report says Simply Voting provides the voting platform, and that once a ballot is stored, no one can determine how an elector voted.

How to take part

  • Check now that your name is on the voters' list at https://stthomas.voterservices.ca. The report says a name must be on the list to vote.
  • If your Voter Information Letter has not arrived by October 7, contact the Voter Help Desk weekdays from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. at 519-631-1680 (press 0) or customerservice@stthomas.ca, or go in person to the City Clerk's Department, Level 2, City Hall, 545 Talbot Street, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

Road closures planned for three November events

Agenda items 11.a, 11.b, 11.c

Council will consider road closures for three events in November:

  • Remembrance Day, Wednesday, November 11: Moore Street between Talbot Street and Centre Street, 8 a.m. to 1 p.m. The St. Thomas Legion, Branch 41 is organizing a ceremony at the new Veterans Memorial Garden, with a police-escorted parade from 24 John St. Access to the Moore Street parking lot from Centre Street would stay open, and staff say a temporary transit detour will be posted in advance.
  • Light up Talbot, Friday, November 13: Mondamin Street from Curtis Street to Talbot Street, and Talbot Street from East Street to Horton Street, 4 to 8:30 p.m. Last year's approved closure was 4:30 to 9:30 p.m. Staff also recommend an exemption from the Animal Control By-law for horse and wagon rides and farm animals for a nativity scene. On the morning of the event, pylons would go up around City Hall along Talbot and Mondamin streets to prohibit on-street parking. A temporary transit detour will be posted in advance.
  • Santa Claus Parade, Saturday, November 21: First Avenue from Talbot Street to Redan Street, 3 to 8:30 p.m., and Talbot Street from First Avenue to William Street, 5:30 to 8:30 p.m. The Optimist Club of St. Thomas sponsors the parade. It runs west along Talbot Street and officially ends at Elgin Street. Parking would be restricted on both sides of the parade route from 5 to 8:30 p.m. Staff recommend that access for emergency vehicles be maintained at all times through the closures, and the east, northbound lane of First Avenue would be available to them.

Last year's (2025) versions of all three events were approved by council in October 2025 by voice vote, after Committee of the Whole; this year's have not been decided. The reports do not give the detour routes.
