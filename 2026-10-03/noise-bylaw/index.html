Council to receive a report on a new Noise Control By-law

Agenda item 19.a

Council will consider a staff recommendation on Monday that report CC-20-26 on a new Noise Control By-law "be received for information" and that the by-law "be brought forward for Council’s consideration." The recommendation does not ask council to adopt the by-law. The draft leaves its effective date blank.

The report calls the City's current noise by-law, adopted in December 2020, "legally valid and enforceable," but says it takes a more traditional approach. Staff list the "Removal of quantitative noise limits and the adoption of qualitative approaches to enforcement." In the draft, whether something is noise turns on an officer's opinion that it "is likely to disturb the reasonable enjoyment of a Premises." It covers sound or vibration "audible at a Point of Reception."

The report says the change "would therefore result in operational cost savings" and "eliminates the need for enforcement staff to use specialized sound level meters." Its cost table, "Cost Breakdown – Quantitative Noise Reader Program," shows $26,393.62 in the first year and $2,400 a year after. It also says "There is no financial impact or additional costs associated with this update."

Under the draft, construction equipment noise that is clearly audible at a point of reception is deemed likely to disturb during restricted times: 9 p.m. to 7 a.m. Monday to Friday, 9 p.m. to 8 a.m. on Saturday, and 8 p.m. to 9 a.m. on Sundays and statutory holidays. While a Heat Warning is in effect, the equipment may start up to one hour earlier. The report says most set fines "have been standardized at $500.00"; it proposes a $250 set fine for persistent barking, calling or whining by a domestic animal. The report does not give the current by-law's hours or fines in the material we reviewed, so we cannot say how these compare.

Questions to ask

  • What numeric noise limits does the City's current noise by-law set, and which of them would the draft Noise Control By-law remove?
  • Does the City already own the equipment covered by the "Cost Breakdown – Quantitative Noise Reader Program" table in report CC-20-26, or would it be a new purchase?
  • If council brings the Noise Control By-law forward, when would it come back to council, and on what date would it take effect? The draft's effective date is blank.
  • What are the current noise by-law's permitted hours and set fines, so they can be compared with the hours in the draft Noise Control By-law and its proposed set fines (most of them $500)?
